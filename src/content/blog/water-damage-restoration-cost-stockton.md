Nobody shopping for water damage restoration is having a good day, and the pricing opacity makes it worse. Here is what jobs actually run in the Stockton area, what moves the number, and where people get overcharged.

## The short answer

<img src="/images/blog/cost-ranges-stockton.svg" alt="Chart of typical water damage restoration cost ranges in Stockton for 2026: one-room dry-out 1,200 to 3,500 dollars, multi-room 3,000 to 7,500, sewage cleanup 5,000 to 15,000 plus, mold remediation 1,500 to 6,000, crawl space 2,000 to 6,000" width="760" height="430" loading="lazy" />

For a typical Stockton home, expect these ranges:

- **Clean water extraction and drying, one room:** $1,200 to $3,500
- **Multiple rooms or flooring involved:** $3,000 to $7,500
- **Gray water (washing machine, dishwasher overflow):** add 30 to 50 percent over clean water rates
- **Sewage or black water cleanup:** $5,000 to $15,000 and up
- **Mold remediation after slow leaks:** $1,500 to $6,000 for contained areas, more if it reached HVAC
- **Full crawl space water removal and drying:** $2,000 to $6,000

These are restoration numbers, not rebuild numbers. Replacing cabinets, drywall, and flooring after the structure is dry is a separate remodel line, and often a separate contractor.

## What actually drives the price

<img src="/images/blog/water-categories.svg" alt="The three categories of water damage: category 1 clean water at baseline cost, category 2 gray water at 1.3 to 1.5 times cost, category 3 black water from sewage at 2 to 3 times cost" width="760" height="260" loading="lazy" />

**Category of water.** Clean supply line water is the cheapest fix. The same gallons from a sewer backup can triple the bill, because everything porous the water touched has to be removed, not dried. This is why a burst pipe caught in an hour is a fundamentally different event than one that ran overnight.

**Time before mitigation started.** Water migrates. In the first hours it is in the carpet. By day two it is in the baseboards, wall cavities, and subfloor. By day four in Valley summer heat, you are pricing mold remediation on top of drying. Every hour of delay is money.

**Square footage of wet material, not room count.** Estimators measure moisture spread with meters. A "small" laundry room leak that wicked into two adjacent walls can cost more than a large but contained kitchen event.

**Construction details.** Stockton housing stock matters here. Older homes near downtown with hardwood over raised foundations dry differently than slab-built tract homes in Spanos Park or Weston Ranch. Raised foundations add crawl space work. Slab homes trap water under flooring layers that have to come up.

## The insurance wrinkle

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe, but not gradual leaks or maintenance failures. Two cost implications:

1. If insurance is paying, mitigation companies bill the carrier at standard Xactimate rates and your out of pocket is the deductible. Get the claim started before signing anything.
2. If you are paying cash, say so up front and ask for the cash price. It is routinely 20 to 40 percent under carrier billing rates.

## Where people get overcharged

- **Assignment of benefits pressure.** A crew that shows up wanting a signature transferring your insurance claim to them before explaining scope. Slow down.
- **Equipment day-count padding.** Drying equipment is billed per day per unit. Legitimate drying for most rooms is 3 to 5 days with daily moisture readings. Ask for the readings.
- **Demolition beyond the moisture line.** Flood cuts should follow the meter, not the crew's schedule.

A reputable operator explains scope in plain language, documents moisture readings, and does not need a signature in the first ten minutes.
